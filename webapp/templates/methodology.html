How this was actually built

Every number on this page is measured, not claimed. This page documents the complete 10-stage pipeline so nothing has to be taken on trust.

1

Data Ingestion & Deduplication

Two yearly Foodpanda Pakistan exports (2025 and 2026) were merged on storeid. The reviews table was deduplicated by uuid — the two exports overlapped by 139,159 reviews, so a naive concatenation would have double-counted 9% of the data. After dedup: 1,552,204 unique reviews across 27 cities and thousands of restaurants, spanning Feb 2024 to Jan 2026.

Restaurant metadata (name, city, food type) was similarly merged by storeid, keeping the most-reviewed entry when duplicates existed. City names were normalized to title case.

2

Language Detection

Every review is classified into one of three language groups before model routing:

  • Urdu Script — detected via Unicode range U+0600–U+06FF (any Arabic/Persian character present)
  • Roman Urdu / Mixed — detected by matching against a 50+ word lexicon of Roman Urdu markers (acha, bohat, zabardast, mazedar, lazeez, behtreen, etc.)
  • English / Other — everything else, the default classification

This flag determines which transformer model handles the review in the ensemble routing step.

4

Dual-Transformer Ensemble Inference

Both transformer models run on every single review — not just a sample. This produces two independent predictions and two confidence scores per review, enabling disagreement-based uncertainty flagging.

Inference runs in GPU-accelerated batches (batch size 128, mixed precision fp16 on CUDA). Progress is checkpointed every 200 batches, so if the Kaggle GPU session times out, it resumes from where it left off rather than restarting.

Text is truncated to 64 tokens — sufficient for food delivery reviews, which are typically 1–3 sentences.

5

Language-Aware Routing

The final sentiment label is determined by routing based on the language flag:

  • Roman Urdu / Mixed → specialist model (Khubaib01) is primary, general model is secondary
  • English / Urdu Script → general model (cardiffnlp) is primary, specialist is secondary

The confidence threshold is set at 0.60. Reviews where models disagree OR where primary confidence drops below this threshold are flagged for LLM review — creating a transparent "uncertainty queue" rather than silently trusting a single prediction.

This means approximately 80% of reviews get high-confidence routed labels, while ~20% are flagged for deeper investigation.

6

Keyword-Based Aspect Extraction

Seven aspect categories are detected using a multilingual keyword dictionary covering both English and Roman Urdu terms:

Taste

tasty, flavour, zaiqa, mazedar, lazeez, namkeen, teekha...

Quantity

portion, kam, chota, size, serving, thori, bara...

Price

expensive, cheap, mehnga, sasta, overpriced, paisa...

Delivery/Rider

late, rider, jaldi, fast, slow, delay, dair, wait...

Packaging

packing, shopper, leak, spilled, torn, container, seal...

Hygiene

dirty, ganda, hair, cockroach, stale, baasi, rotten...

Service

behavior, staff, rude, polite, attitude, professional, instructions...

Text is normalized before matching: repeated characters are collapsed (boooht → boht) and non-alpha characters are stripped. Approximately 55% of reviews match at least one aspect keyword.

7

Zero-Shot Aspect Discovery

The keyword method has a real "blind spot" — reviews with zero aspect keywords. To discover what it misses, a cross-lingual NLI model was run on a 10,000-review sample of these blind-spot reviews:

MoritzLaurer/mDeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-xnli multi_label=True confidence ≥ 0.5

This model was chosen because mDeBERTa-v3 is trained on multilingual NLI data (100+ languages) and can classify text into arbitrary candidate labels without fine-tuning. Candidate labels included both existing aspect categories and new ones like "wrong item received", "refund or payment issue", "food arrived cold", and "app or ordering technical issue".

This ran on 10k reviews (not the full 1.5M dataset) — as a student with Kaggle GPU limits, this was a scoped proof of concept to show what to add to the keyword list next.

8

Unsupervised Topic Modeling (BERTopic)

To discover organic topic clusters without any predefined labels, 100,000 reviews were embedded with paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 and clustered using BERTopic.

A custom stopword list combined English stopwords with 50+ Roman Urdu function words (acha, bohat, hai, tha, kya, etc.) to prevent them from dominating topic labels. The vectorizer used bigram features with min_df=10.

The model discovered dozens of organic topic clusters — some confirming known aspects (taste complaints, delivery issues), others revealing patterns neither the keyword list nor zero-shot classifier anticipated.

9

SHAP Explainability

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was applied to explain individual predictions at the word level. For each review, SHAP shows which specific words pushed the model toward positive, negative, or neutral — making the transformer's decisions interpretable rather than opaque.

SHAP was run specifically on the LLM review queue (reviews where models disagreed or confidence was low) to understand why the ensemble was uncertain. Key findings: Roman Urdu function words like "hai", "tha", "mein" were major confusion sources for the general model, while sentiment-bearing words like "zabardast", "bakwas", "lazeez" were correctly weighted by the specialist model.

A word-level masker with a regex tokenizer (\b\w+\b) was used, with fallback handling for very short or emoji-only reviews that SHAP couldn't tokenize.

10

LLM Review Queue & Aggregation

Reviews flagged by the routing step (models disagree OR confidence < 0.60) form a prioritized review queue — exported as llm_review_queue.csv. These are the reviews where a more capable LLM (like GPT-4 or Groq) could add value, and the web platform's AI assistant uses this same queue logic.

All results are aggregated at multiple levels: city summaries (sentiment mix, avg star rating, top complaint, reliability scores), restaurant summaries (per-store sentiment, aspect breakdowns, top complaint/praise), and national aspect summaries (total mentions, negative rate per aspect).

Temporal trends are computed monthly — tracking positive/negative/neutral percentages over time, with a 3-month rolling average to identify months where sentiment deviated significantly.

City reliability scores measure model agreement rate by region — checking whether the pipeline works equally well across Pakistan's diverse linguistic landscape.

Known limitations, stated plainly

• Neither model reaches "solved" accuracy — 67.9% on Roman Urdu is a real improvement over a general model's 48.8%, not a finished result.

• Both models measurably over-predict "negative" on the benchmark, which likely inflates the platform-wide negative percentage somewhat beyond the true rate.

• Zero-shot aspect discovery ran on a 10k-review sample, not the full dataset, due to student compute constraints on Kaggle GPU.

• BERTopic ran on 100k reviews — representative but not exhaustive.

• The keyword-based aspect list is a fixed vocabulary — it has a real, measured false-negative rate even for categories it does try to detect.

• Urdu Script reviews are routed to the general multilingual model, which wasn't specifically benchmarked on Urdu script food reviews.

• Star ratings and text sentiment often diverge — a 5-star review can contain negative text (e.g., "food was good but delivery was terrible"), and the pipeline captures this nuance.
